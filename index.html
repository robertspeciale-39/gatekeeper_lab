Read the ACL

A firewall checks every packet against its access control list, top to bottom, and the first rule that matches decides. Click the rule that fires. No match at all means the packet is denied.

Anatomy of a rule

3.4.B · 3.4.D
ALLOW inbound TCP port 22 from ALL;
ALLOW

Action. Permit or deny matching traffic.

inbound

Direction. Entering or leaving the network.

TCP port 22

Criteria. Protocol and port (SSH here). Can also be a service or application.

from ALL

Source. One IP, a range like 192.168.1.0/24, or ALL. Add to to limit the destination.

Three habits for every ACL

3.4.B
  1. Read top to bottom. Rules are checked in order.
  2. First match wins. Once a rule matches, the rest are ignored. Reordering can flip the result.
  3. No match means deny. Real firewalls end every ACL with an implicit deny. BEYOND CED

Direction: in this app, inbound means heading toward the more trusted side of that firewall, and outbound means heading toward the less trusted side.
